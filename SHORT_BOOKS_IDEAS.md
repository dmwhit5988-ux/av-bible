# Short books (1–3 chapters) — per-chapter visual ideas

> **Status (2026-08-10):** **2 Peter and Zephaniah are built** — 6 chapters,
> 114 verses, `generate_2peter_svg.py` and `generate_zephaniah_svg.py`, with
> the marks they share factored into `svg_vocab.py`. The other eleven books
> are unbuilt.
>
> Changes made while building 2 Peter: v19's liberty-and-bondage reversal
> needed its own frame rather than sharing one with the wells and clouds, and
> the chapter-2 precedents carry their survivor counts as numerals under the
> rail rather than inside the panels. For Zephaniah: 1:3's four categories are
> struck creature by creature rather than one specimen standing for each, and
> the four refusals of 3:2 are labelled in a row rather than radially, where
> the office panels left no room for their names.
>
> ⚠️ **Translation.** The shipped graphics use **WEB** wording throughout —
> that is the project's translation (`config.json`), and the visuals are
> translation-generic. The quotations in the design notes below are AV and are
> illustrative only; check the WEB text before lettering anything from them.
> Several labels differ materially: WEB has *moral excellence / self-control /
> perseverance / brotherly affection / love* on the 2 Peter 1 ladder, *Beor*
> not *Bosor*, *Noah with seven others* not *Noah the eighth person*, *Yahweh*
> not *the LORD*, *Cush* not *Ethiopia*, and *pelican and porcupine* not
> *cormorant and bittern*. Chapter and verse divisions are unaffected.

Every book in the AV canon with **three chapters or fewer**: 13 books, 28 chapters.
Six in the Old Testament, seven in the New.

These are the books the catalog (`docs/content-catalog.md`) mostly waves off with a
one-line "low density" note. That verdict was made looking for *maps and timelines*.
Read instead for **contrast pairs, ordered sequences, ledgers, and thresholds**, and
most of them turn out to be well-suited — several are stronger per-chapter subjects
than the long books they sit next to, because a whole book fits in one world and the
camera never has to leave it.

Design rules assumed throughout (per the house style): the verse is being narrated,
so **no caption lines and no prose on the graphic** — names, numbers and labelled
drawings only. One world per chapter, revealed by a per-verse camera.

Type icons follow `docs/content-catalog.md`:
🗺️ map · ⏳ timeline · 🌳 genealogy · 🏛️ architectural · 🔄 process/cycle ·
📊 comparison · 🔢 numeric · 💡 symbolic

## Old Testament

### Obadiah — 1 chapter

**Obadiah (21 vv.)** · 💡 + 🗺️ — *one cliff, drawn in section*

The chapter has a single governing axis: **height**. Edom lives "in the clefts of
the rock," sets its nest "among the stars," and every judgment line is a descent.
Draw one cliff face in cross-section, Edom's eyrie near the top, and let the camera
fall down it as the oracle proceeds.

- **vv. 1–4** — the nest among the stars, and the line that brings it down. An
  altitude gauge on the margin is the whole progress indicator; no counter needed.
- **vv. 5–7** — the gleaning logic: thieves leave something, grape-gatherers leave
  something, Esau is left nothing. Three small harvest bins, two with a remainder,
  the third scraped clean. The confederate allies then drawn on the wrong side of
  the border line.
- **vv. 8–9** — the wise men of Teman and the mighty of Bozrah struck off by name.
- **vv. 10–14** — **the crossway (v. 14) is the set-piece.** Eight successive
  "thou shouldest not have…" clauses, each a specific act at a specific moment:
  looking on, rejoicing, entering the gate, laying hands on substance, standing in
  the crossway to cut off the escaping. Draw a literal fork in the road with Edom
  posted at it and the fugitive lines running through — each verse marks one more
  refused exit. This is the emotional centre and it is genuinely diagrammatic.
- **vv. 15–18** — the cup passed round the heathen; Jacob fire, Joseph flame, Esau
  stubble — three labelled materials, one outcome.
- **vv. 19–21** — the chapter turns into an actual **map**: the south possesses
  mount Esau, the plain the Philistines, the fields of Ephraim and Samaria,
  Benjamin takes Gilead, the captivity at Zarephath and Sepharad comes back for the
  cities of the south. The cliff view widens out to a territory diagram, saviours
  standing on Zion opposite the emptied crag.

### Haggai — 2 chapters

The most date-stamped short book in the canon — five oracles, each pinned to a day
of Darius' second year. **A date rail runs the length of both chapters** and is the
book's spine; the two chapters simply place different drawings above it.

**Haggai 1 (15 vv.)** · 📊 + 🏛️ — *two houses and a bag with a hole*

- **v. 1** — first pin on the rail: sixth month, first day.
- **vv. 2–4** — two buildings side by side at the same scale: a cieled, panelled
  private house, finished; the LORD's house, a bare foundation. This pairing holds
  the whole chapter.
- **vv. 5–6** — **the futility ledger, the best single graphic in Haggai 1.** Five
  rows, each an input that fails to arrive at its output: sown much / brought in
  little; eat / not enough; drink / not filled; clothe / not warm; earn wages / a
  bag with holes. Draw the bag with the hole and let the coins fall through. Five
  rows, no sentences.
- **vv. 7–11** — the timber route up the mountain; the drought called down on corn,
  new wine, oil, men, cattle, and "all the labour of the hands" — the same ledger
  rows, now struck out at source.
- **vv. 12–15** — Zerubbabel, Joshua the high priest and the remnant obey; second
  pin on the rail, the 24th day, 23 days after the first. The foundation begins to
  carry courses.

**Haggai 2 (23 vv.)** · 💡 + 🔄 — *the ghosted first temple, and a contact chain*

- **vv. 1–9** — seventh month, 21st day. "Who is left among you that saw this house
  in her first glory?" Draw Solomon's temple **ghosted behind the new work, at true
  relative scale**, so the shortfall is seen rather than stated; then the shaking of
  heavens, earth, sea and dry land, and the silver and gold marked as already owned.
  The latter house's outline finally overtops the ghost.
- **vv. 10–14** — ninth month, 24th day. **The priests' two rulings are a real
  asymmetry diagram, and the standout idea in the book.** Ruling one: holy flesh
  carried in a skirt touching bread, pottage, wine, oil — nothing is made holy.
  Ruling two: a man defiled by a body touches any of the same — all are defiled.
  Draw one contact chain twice: holiness stops dead after the first link, uncleanness
  propagates down every link. Two rows of the same four objects, one row staying
  plain, the other going dark all the way along.
- **vv. 15–19** — the shortfall numbers: a heap of twenty yielding ten, a press
  expected to draw fifty yielding twenty. Two measure-jars each drawn twice, actual
  against expected; blasting, mildew and hail marked on the rows. Then the pivot —
  "from this day will I bless you" — and the jars fill.
- **vv. 20–23** — same day, second word: thrones overthrown, chariots and horses
  down, every one by the sword of his brother; closing on the signet ring, drawn
  small and alone after the wreckage.

### Joel — 3 chapters

One landscape serves all three chapters: a field, a city on a rise behind it, a
valley below. Chapter 1 strips it, chapter 2 assaults and then restores it, chapter
3 empties the nations into the valley.

**Joel 1 (20 vv.)** · 🔄 + 🔢 — *four passes over one field*

- **v. 4** — the four-stage chain: palmerworm → locust → cankerworm → caterpiller,
  each eating what the last one left. Four passes across the same field, the ground
  a stage barer each time. This is the chapter's clock.
- **vv. 5–12** — the produce ledger going out crop by crop as it is named: vine,
  fig tree (barked white), pomegranate, palm, apple, wheat, barley. Each named crop
  is one row that empties on its own verse.
- **vv. 9–13** — the temple with the meat offering and the drink offering visibly
  cut off; the priests in sackcloth.
- **vv. 14–20** — the solemn assembly called; the seed rotten under the clods, the
  barns broken down, the garners laid desolate, the beasts perplexed for want of
  pasture, the rivers of water dried up. The landscape's last colours drain out.

**Joel 2 (32 vv.)** · 💡 + 🔄 — *the swarm becomes an army, then the ledger refills*

- **vv. 1–11** — the trumpet in Zion; the same swarm redrawn **in ranks** — running
  like horsemen, leaping on the tops of mountains, not breaking rank, not thrusting
  one another, climbing the wall, entering in at the windows like a thief. Draw the
  city wall in elevation and the column going up it in formation. Sun, moon and
  stars withdraw above.
- **vv. 12–17** — the turn. "Rend your heart, and not your garments." The trumpet
  sounds a second time and now it **gathers** instead of alarms: a summons strip of
  who is called, in the text's own order — elders, children, those that suck the
  breasts, the bridegroom out of his chamber, the bride out of her closet, the
  priests between the porch and the altar. Six figures taking their places.
- **vv. 18–27** — **restoration read directly off chapter 1's ledger.** The rain
  comes, the floors fill with wheat, the fats overflow with wine and oil — and
  "I will restore to you the years that the locust hath eaten" refills the four bars
  the four insects emptied, in reverse order of the eating. The single best payoff
  in the book, and it costs nothing because the ledger is already drawn.
- **vv. 28–32** — the Spirit poured out: sons and daughters prophesy, old men dream,
  young men see visions, servants and handmaids included — four labelled groups
  under one downpour. Blood, fire and pillars of smoke below; sun to darkness and
  moon to blood above; the escape on Zion marked.

**Joel 3 (21 vv.)** · 🗺️ + 📊 — *the valley of decision*

- **vv. 1–8** — the basin of Jehoshaphat as the assembly floor. Around it, a trade
  ring: Tyre, Zidon, all the coasts of Palestine, the Grecians, the Sabeans, with
  arrows showing where the children were sold and what for (a boy for a harlot, a
  girl for wine). Then **every arrow reverses** — sold back into the hand of Judah.
  A traffic diagram run backwards.
- **vv. 9–15** — "Beat your plowshares into swords and your pruninghooks into
  spears": two tools drawn mid-conversion, the famous line inverted. The weak saying
  "I am strong." Multitudes funnelling into the valley; the harvest sickle and the
  full winepress; sun, moon and stars dark again.
- **vv. 16–21** — the reversal completes: the mountains drop new wine, the hills
  flow with milk, the rivers of Judah flow with waters, and a fountain comes out of
  the house of the LORD to water the valley of Shittim. Egypt and Edom drawn as the
  two dry patches left on an otherwise watered map.

### Nahum — 3 chapters

One city, approached, entered, and compared to a city that already fell. The three
chapters are a natural approach → assault → verdict progression.

**Nahum 1 (15 vv.)** · 💡 + 📊 — *a storm front, and one rock in it*

- **vv. 1–6** — the theophany as weather: whirlwind and storm, the clouds the dust
  of his feet. Bashan, Carmel and Lebanon drawn as three named ridges that wither in
  turn as the front crosses them — the landscape is the progress bar.
- **vv. 7–11** — the chapter's hinge, and a clean two-column ledger: "a strong hold
  in the day of trouble… he knoweth them that trust in him" against "with an
  overrunning flood he will make an utter end." One rock standing in rising water,
  people on it; everything else level with the flood.
- **vv. 12–15** — the yoke broken and the bonds burst; the graven image cut off out
  of the house of the gods. Closing on **the feet upon the mountains** — good tidings
  arriving from the same direction the storm came.
- *(Optional structure note: vv. 2–8 are a partial Hebrew alphabetic acrostic. If the
  Psalm 119 / Lamentations acrostic treatment ever gets built, the letter ticks could
  be reused here as a quiet margin device.)*

**Nahum 2 (13 vv.)** · 🏛️ + 🔄 — *the city in cutaway, taken piece by piece*

The most cinematic chapter in the twelve minor prophets, and it maps onto a single
architectural cutaway: wall, river gates, palace, treasury, and the lions' den behind.

- **vv. 1–5** — the defence mounted: keep the munition, watch the way, make loins
  strong. Then the attackers — shields red, men in scarlet, chariots with flaming
  torches justling one against another in the broad ways, running like lightnings.
  Draw the streets in plan and let the chariot tracks scribble across them.
- **vv. 6–7** — **"The gates of the rivers shall be opened, and the palace shall be
  dissolved."** The river gate opens on its own verse and the water takes the palace
  section of the drawing. Huzzab led away, the maids mourning as doves.
- **vv. 8–10** — "Nineveh is of old like a pool of water, yet they shall flee away":
  the pool draining. Then the spoil ledger — silver, gold, "none end of the store" —
  counted up and then emptied to nothing across three words: empty, void, waste.
- **vv. 11–13** — the closing image needs no text at all: **the lions' den, drawn
  empty.** The lion, the old lion, the lion's whelp, the feeding place — all labelled,
  all vacant, the torn prey gone.

**Nahum 3 (19 vv.)** · 📊 — *Nineveh set beside Thebes*

- **vv. 1–7** — the bloody city: the horseman's bright sword and glittering spear,
  the heap of corpses. The harlot mistress of witchcrafts, nations and families sold;
  the shame spectacle.
- **vv. 8–10** — **the spine of the chapter: "Art thou better than populous No?"**
  Two city profiles side by side, each with the identical row of defences and allies
  listed beneath — situate among the rivers, waters round about, the sea her rampart,
  Ethiopia and Egypt her strength, Put and Lubim her helpers. Thebes' rows are already
  struck through; Nineveh's strike through one at a time as the chapter proceeds. A
  direct, honest comparison chart the text itself sets up.
- **vv. 11–15** — the strongholds as fig trees with the firstripe figs: shaken, they
  fall into the eater's mouth. The gates set wide open, the bars devoured by fire.
  "Draw thee waters for the siege, fortify thy strong holds… make strong the brickkiln"
  — preparations drawn while the wall behind is already breached.
- **vv. 16–19** — the crowned ones and the captains as locusts camping in the hedges
  in the cold day; when the sun rises they **flee away and their place is not known**.
  The swarm that opened Joel closes Nahum, departing. The wound with no healing; all
  that hear the report clapping.

### Habakkuk — 3 chapters

A dialogue, a watchtower, and a march. Three completely different geometries — the
best per-chapter variety of any short book.

**Habakkuk 1 (17 vv.)** · 💡 📊 — *a two-sided exchange*

Use the **speaker-strip-with-arcs device already built for Genesis 3**: every line
drawn as an arc from speaker to hearer, arcs persisting so the exchange reads whole.
Here there are only two speakers and four moves.

- **vv. 2–4** — first complaint: how long? Spoiling and violence, the law slacked,
  judgment going forth wrong, the wicked compassing the righteous. Draw the crooked
  judgment as a bent line where a straight one is expected.
- **vv. 5–11** — the answer, and a nasty little attribute chart: the Chaldeans'
  horses swifter than leopards, fiercer than the evening wolves, flying as the eagle
  that hasteth to eat. Three animals labelled with the one quality each contributes.
  They gather captivity as the sand, scoff at kings, heap dust and take strongholds.
- **vv. 12–17** — second complaint, and the image that carries it: **men made as the
  fishes of the sea, taken up with the angle, gathered in the drag** — and the fisher
  then sacrificing to his net and burning incense to his drag. Draw the net hauling,
  and an altar with a net on it. The chapter ends unanswered, on the hook.

**Habakkuk 2 (20 vv.)** · 🏛️ + 📊 — *the tower, the tablet, five woes cut into it*

- **vv. 1–4** — the watchtower, drawn as the vantage the whole chapter is seen from.
  "**Write the vision, and make it plain upon tables, that he may run that readeth
  it**" — the book asks for an infographic in so many words; the tablet is the surface
  everything else in the chapter is inscribed on.
- **vv. 5–19** — **five woes, five stanzas, five emblems cut into the tablet**, one
  per turn of the camera:
  1. him that increaseth that which is not his — a stack of pledges, the creditors
     rising up to bite;
  2. him that coveteth an evil covetousness, to set his nest on high — the nest again,
     answering Obadiah;
  3. him that buildeth a town with blood — a foundation course laid in red, with
     **the stone crying out of the wall and the beam answering it** (v. 11), drawn
     literally as two marked members of the same building;
  4. him that giveth his neighbour drink — the cup, and the LORD's right hand cup
     turned back on him;
  5. him that saith to the wood, Awake — the dumb idol overlaid with gold and silver,
     with no breath in it.
- **v. 14** — between the woes, the widening flood: "the earth shall be filled with
  the knowledge of the glory of the LORD, **as the waters cover the sea**." The one
  bright interruption; let it wash across the tablet.
- **v. 20** — everything goes quiet: the holy temple, and every figure in the chapter
  silent before it.

**Habakkuk 3 (19 vv.)** · 🗺️ + 🔢 — *a march, and six empty stalls*

- **vv. 1–2** — the psalm heading (Shigionoth, and the closing "to the chief singer
  on my stringed instruments") frames the chapter as sung; a plain staff or string
  motif at the margin is enough to mark it.
- **vv. 3–15** — a **route across named ground**: God from Teman, the Holy One from
  mount Paran; horns coming out of his hand, pestilence before him and burning coals
  at his feet; the everlasting mountains scattered, the tents of Cushan in affliction,
  the curtains of Midian trembling; the rivers, the sea, and the sun and moon standing
  still in their habitation. Draw the named places as a march line, with each element
  of the landscape reacting on its own verse.
- **vv. 17–19** — **the ending is the best "less is more" graphic in the book.** Six
  labelled rows, every one at zero: the fig tree not blossoming, no fruit in the vines,
  the labour of the olive failing, the fields yielding no meat, the flock cut off from
  the fold, no herd in the stalls. Draw six empty stalls and bins, all counted, all
  nothing — then one figure on hinds' feet, up on the high places above them. No words
  needed beyond the six labels.

### Zephaniah — 3 chapters ✅ Built

**Zephaniah 1 (18 vv.)** · 💡 + 🗺️ — *creation run backwards, then a lantern through the city*

- **vv. 2–3** — **the sweep list runs Genesis 1's creation order in reverse**: man,
  beast, fowls of the heaven, fishes of the sea. The Genesis 1 graphic is already
  built as a forming-then-filling chart — **invert it.** Four bands emptying from the
  top down, in the exact reverse of the order they were filled. The strongest
  cross-book idea in this whole file.
- **vv. 4–6** — the targets named: the remnant of Baal, the Chemarims, them that
  worship the host of heaven upon the housetops, them that swear by the LORD and by
  Malcham, them that are turned back. Rooftops drawn along the skyline, each marked.
- **vv. 7–13** — the day of the LORD as a **sacrifice with guests bidden**, and then a
  search of the city by quarter: the fish gate, the second, the hills, Maktesh (the
  mortar), the merchant people. Draw Jerusalem in plan and move a lantern through it —
  "I will search Jerusalem with candles" — quarter by quarter, finding the men settled
  on their lees. Houses built but not inhabited, vineyards planted but not drunk of:
  two more empty-ledger rows.
- **vv. 14–18** — the word ladder that became the *Dies Irae*: wrath, trouble,
  distress, wasteness, desolation, darkness, gloominess, clouds, thick darkness,
  trumpet and alarm. Ten steps, each one darker than the last — a bar that goes to
  black one word at a time. Silver and gold explicitly unable to deliver.

**Zephaniah 2 (15 vv.)** · 🗺️ — *a compass laid over Judah*

The cleanest map chapter in the short books, because the oracles go round the points
of the compass in order.

- **vv. 1–3** — the meek of the earth gathered at the centre before the circuit starts.
- **vv. 4–7** — **west:** the four Philistine cities in a row — Gaza forsaken,
  Ashkelon a desolation, Ashdod driven out at the noon day, Ekron rooted up. The
  seacoast then redrawn as pasture with cottages and folds for the remnant of Judah.
- **vv. 8–11** — **east:** Moab as Sodom, Ammon as Gomorrah — the breeding of nettles,
  saltpits, a perpetual desolation. Two ruined sites, labelled with their precedents.
- **v. 12** — **south:** the Ethiopians, one line, one mark.
- **vv. 13–15** — **north:** Assyria and Nineveh made dry like a wilderness. Flocks
  lying down in the midst of her, the cormorant and the bittern lodging in the upper
  lintels, singing in the windows, desolation in the thresholds. Draw the ruin with
  the birds occupying the named architectural parts — lintel, window, threshold, cedar
  work — a building labelled by its squatters.

**Zephaniah 3 (20 vv.)** · 📊 + 💡 — *four offices, failed and refilled*

- **vv. 1–7** — the four offices of the city, each with its own image: her princes
  roaring lions, her judges evening wolves, her prophets light and treacherous, her
  priests polluting the sanctuary. Four panels, four emblems, all four struck. Set
  against them, at the centre, "the just LORD is in the midst thereof… every morning
  doth he bring his judgment to light, he faileth not" — a morning tick that keeps
  arriving on schedule while the four fail.
- **vv. 8–13** — the turn: a pure language given, suppliants coming from beyond the
  rivers of Ethiopia bringing an offering — arrows now running **inward** on the
  compass map of chapter 2, from the same points that were struck. The afflicted and
  poor remnant feeding and lying down with none making them afraid.
- **vv. 14–20** — the four panels refill, and the same phrase "in the midst of thee"
  comes back for the last time — this time the centre figure is not judging but
  **singing**: "he will rest in his love, he will joy over thee with singing." The
  gathering closes it: her that halteth saved, her that was driven out gathered, the
  captivity turned back before their eyes.

## New Testament

### Philemon — 1 chapter

**Philemon (25 vv.)** · 💡 + 🔢 + 🗺️ — *three people, one route, one ledger*

The catalog currently reads "no infographic candidates." It has three, and they fit
on one page.

- **vv. 1–7** — the cast, drawn as a small relationship diagram rather than a list:
  Paul (a prisoner — draw the chain, it is referenced four times), Timothy, Philemon,
  Apphia, Archippus, and **the church in thy house** — so the house is the frame the
  whole chapter sits inside.
- **vv. 8–16** — **the status change is the graphic.** One figure, Onesimus, with the
  label on him changing on its own verse: *unprofitable* → *profitable* (the name
  itself means useful — the pun is the argument), then *servant* → *above a servant,
  a brother beloved*. The arrow between Onesimus and Philemon is redrawn each time.
  Beneath it, the round trip: Colossae → Rome (a runaway, going one way) → Colossae
  (carrying this letter, going back).
- **vv. 17–19** — **the ledger, and the best single idea in the chapter.** "If he
  hath wronged thee, or oweth thee ought, put that on mine account… I Paul have
  written it with mine own hand, I will repay it." Draw a two-column account with the
  debt transferred across from Onesimus to Paul, in Paul's own hand. Then the counter-
  entry that swallows it: "albeit I do not say to thee how thou owest unto me even
  thine own self besides" — an unbilled line beneath, larger than the debt above it.
- **vv. 20–25** — the guest chamber prepared, and the greeting list: Epaphras,
  Marcus, Aristarchus, Demas, Lucas. Five names round the house that opened the page.

### 2 John — 1 chapter

**2 John (13 vv.)** · 💡 — *a threshold, rightly closed*

The whole letter is about **what crosses a doorway.** Draw the elect lady's house
with one door and put everything at it.

- **vv. 1–6** — truth and love arriving together and going in: "not as though I wrote
  a new commandment unto thee, but that which we had from the beginning" — an arrow
  running back down the timeline rather than forward, which is the letter's one
  temporal move.
- **vv. 7–9** — the deceivers "who confess not that Jesus Christ is come in the
  flesh" approaching the door. "Look to yourselves, that we lose not those things
  which we have wrought" — the things already wrought drawn inside, at risk.
- **vv. 10–11** — the door shut. Receive him not into your house, neither bid him
  God speed. This is the image the letter exists for; hold the camera on it.
- **vv. 12–13** — paper and ink set aside for **face to face** — the closing emblem,
  shared with 3 John (see below).

### 3 John — 1 chapter

**3 John (14 vv.)** · 📊 + 💡 — *three doorways, and one wrongly bolted*

**Design 2 and 3 John as a deliberate diptych** — the same threshold drawn twice, in
opposite senses. It is the cheapest pairing in the file and the most satisfying.

- Three doorways side by side, one per named man, each resolving on its own verses:
  - **Gaius (vv. 1–8)** — open. He receives the brethren and strangers, and brings
    them forward on their journey. Draw the travelling brethren "taking nothing of
    the Gentiles," so the supply line has to come from inside the door — a small
    supply arrow that only works if the door stands open.
  - **Diotrephes (vv. 9–11)** — bolted, and this is the wrong one. He "loveth to have
    the preeminence," receives us not, prates with malicious words, will not receive
    the brethren himself, **forbids them that would**, and casts them out of the
    church. Two arrows blocked and one person thrown back through the door.
  - **Demetrius (v. 12)** — a clean report over the lintel: "of all men, and of the
    truth itself."
- **vv. 13–14** — same ending as 2 John: paper and ink laid down, face to face, "our
  friends salute thee. Greet the friends by name." Draw the two letters' endings
  identically so the pair closes together.

### Jude — 1 chapter

**Jude (25 vv.)** · 📊 + 💡 + 🌳 — *triads, and six things failing at their own job*

Densest single chapter in the file. Jude thinks in threes, so the layout can too.

- **vv. 3–4** — the contention for the faith; certain men crept in unawares.
- **vv. 5–7** — **three judgment precedents, one rail**: the people saved out of
  Egypt then destroyed; the angels which kept not their first estate, reserved in
  everlasting chains under darkness; Sodom and Gomorrha set forth for an example.
  Three panels in time order.
- **vv. 8–11** — Michael contending with the devil about the body of Moses (drawn as
  a disputed object between two figures, and nothing more); then **the second triad**:
  the way of Cain, the error of Balaam, the gainsaying of Core — three routes leading
  off one road, each labelled with its man.
- **vv. 12–13** — **the set-piece, and the purest "labelled drawings, no prose"
  passage in the whole file.** Six things drawn failing at the one thing they exist to
  do:
  1. clouds without water, carried about of winds — no rain falls from them;
  2. trees whose fruit withereth, without fruit, twice dead, plucked up by the roots
     — drawn upended, roots in the air;
  3. raging waves of the sea, foaming out their own shame — motion that produces only
     foam;
  4. wandering stars — a star with no fixed course, drifting off the chart into the
     blackness of darkness reserved for it.
  (Six images across four clauses; give each its own beat of camera.)
- **vv. 14–16** — **Enoch, "the seventh from Adam"** — a generation count, so pull
  the seven nodes straight out of the already-built Genesis 5 graphic and light the
  seventh. A free cross-book link, and it makes the prophecy's antiquity visible
  instead of stated.
- **vv. 17–23** — the four instructions (build up, pray, keep, look) and then a
  **three-way triage**, which is a genuine process diagram: "of some have compassion,
  making a difference: and others save with fear, pulling them out of the fire; hating
  even the garment spotted by the flesh." Three cases, three different responses, one
  of them handled at arm's length.
- **vv. 24–25** — closing on "him that is able to keep you from falling" — every
  falling thing in the chapter (angels, stars, trees, men) answered by one that does
  not.

### Titus — 3 chapters

**Titus 1 (16 vv.)** · 🗺️ + 📊 — *an island, city by city*

- **vv. 1–5** — Crete drawn as a real island map: "that thou shouldest set in order
  the things that are wanting, and **ordain elders in every city**." City dots along
  the coast, each waiting for a marker, each filled as the chapter proceeds.
- **vv. 6–9** — the qualification list as a two-sided checklist against one figure:
  on one side the disqualifiers (selfwilled, soon angry, given to wine, a striker,
  given to filthy lucre), on the other the requirements (blameless, husband of one
  wife, faithful children, a lover of hospitality, a lover of good men, sober, just,
  holy, temperate, holding fast the faithful word). Names and words only, no sentences.
- **vv. 10–16** — the counterweight: many unruly and vain talkers, "whose mouths must
  be stopped, who subvert whole houses" — draw the houses being subverted against the
  cities being ordered, the same island read two ways. The Cretan proverb quoted by
  one of their own; "unto the pure all things are pure" as the closing test.

**Titus 2 (15 vv.)** · 📊 + ⏳ — *the household table, and two appearings*

- **vv. 1–10** — **a five-row table, and it is already a table in the text.** Aged
  men, aged women, young women, young men, servants — each row carrying its charge
  and, crucially, its **purpose clause**, which the other household codes don't spell
  out this consistently: *that the word of God be not blasphemed*, *that he that is
  of the contrary part may be ashamed*, *that they may adorn the doctrine of God our
  Saviour in all things*. Five groups, five charges, three reasons — one clean grid.
- **vv. 11–15** — **the two appearings**, drawn as a bracket: the grace of God that
  *hath appeared* behind, the glorious appearing ahead, and "this present world"
  between them, with the three adverbs — soberly, righteously, godly — placed inside
  the span. A timeline only three marks long, which is exactly the right size.

**Titus 3 (15 vv.)** · 📊 + 🔄 — *were / now, with a washing at the pivot*

- **vv. 1–2** — the civic row: subject to principalities, ready to every good work,
  speak evil of no man, no brawlers, gentle, showing meekness unto all men.
- **vv. 3–7** — **the before/after, hinged on one word.** Left column, "we ourselves
  also were sometimes": foolish, disobedient, deceived, serving divers lusts, living
  in malice and envy, hateful, hating one another. Then *but after that the kindness
  and love of God our Saviour toward man appeared* — the pivot, drawn as the washing
  of regeneration, "not by works of righteousness which we have done." Right column
  fills: justified by his grace, heirs according to the hope of eternal life. Two
  columns, one basin between them.
- **vv. 8–11** — what to avoid, listed flat: foolish questions, genealogies,
  contentions, strivings about the law. Then a small **two-strike process diagram**:
  first admonition, second admonition, reject. Three steps, drawn as three.
- **vv. 12–15** — the logistics coda: Artemas or Tychicus sent, winter at Nicopolis,
  Zenas the lawyer and Apollos brought on their journey. A short route strip and four
  names — the island map from chapter 1, zoomed out one notch.

### 2 Thessalonians — 3 chapters

**2 Thessalonians 1 (12 vv.)** · 📊 — *a balance that tips at the revealing*

- **vv. 3–4** — two rising bars: faith growing exceedingly, charity abounding. Drawn
  small, at the top, as the measured facts of the report.
- **vv. 5–10** — **the recompense ledger, and it is literally a balance.** On one
  pan, those who trouble you; on the other, you who are troubled. At the moment of
  the revealing — the Lord from heaven with his mighty angels, in flaming fire — the
  loads **swap sides**: tribulation to the troublers, rest to the troubled. Draw one
  beam, two pans, and let the verse tip it.
- **vv. 11–12** — the counting worthy, and the name glorified in his saints; the fire
  from v. 8 resolving into the glory of v. 10 rather than being a second image.

**2 Thessalonians 2 (17 vv.)** · ⏳ + 🔄 — *a gated sequence with one door held shut*

The strongest diagram in the short-book NT: a strict ordering the text insists on.

- **vv. 1–3** — "**that day shall not come, except there come a falling away first**"
  — the whole chapter is an ordering claim, so draw it as gates on a rail that cannot
  open out of turn.
- **vv. 3–7** — the gates in order: the falling away → **he who now letteth, until he
  be taken out of the way** (draw the restrainer as a bar across the door, the one
  element holding the sequence still) → the man of sin revealed, sitting in the temple
  of God, showing himself that he is God.
- **vv. 8–12** — the last gate opens and closes at once: consumed with the spirit of
  his mouth, destroyed with the brightness of his coming. Beside it, the false
  apparatus — power, signs and lying wonders, strong delusion, believing a lie —
  drawn as a counterfeit of the real signs elsewhere in the rail.
- **vv. 13–17** — the counter-sequence for the readers, running the other way from
  the beginning: chosen from the beginning, through sanctification of the Spirit and
  belief of the truth, called by the gospel, to the obtaining of glory. Two rails,
  opposite directions.

**2 Thessalonians 3 (18 vv.)** · 🔄 + 💡 — *two walks, and a signature*

- **The whole-chapter bracket:** the letter opens by warning against "a letter as
  from us" (2:2) and closes with "**the salutation of Paul with mine own hand, which
  is the token in every epistle: so I write**" (3:17). A forged letter at one end, a
  verified hand at the other — draw the seal/signature as the closing emblem and it
  answers something set up two chapters earlier.
- **vv. 6–12** — two walking tracks side by side. The ordered walk: the tradition
  received, and Paul's own night-and-day labour, eating no man's bread for nought.
  The disorderly walk: working not at all, but busybodies. Between them the rule,
  stated as a rule and drawn as a gate — "**if any would not work, neither should he
  eat**."
- **vv. 13–15** — the discipline drawn as **distance, not expulsion**: note that man
  and have no company with him — "yet count him not as an enemy, but admonish him as
  a brother." He moves further out on the diagram; he does not leave it. Worth being
  precise about, because most treatments get it wrong.

### 2 Peter — 3 chapters ✅ Built

**2 Peter 1 (21 vv.)** · 📊 + 💡 — *a ladder, and how far it lets you see*

- **vv. 5–7** — **the ladder**, already flagged in the catalog: faith → virtue →
  knowledge → temperance → patience → godliness → brotherly kindness → charity. Eight
  rungs, each added on its own clause. The chapter's spine.
- **vv. 8–11** — the payoff drawn as **range of sight**: he that lacketh these things
  is blind, "and cannot see afar off." Put a sight-line gauge beside the ladder — the
  higher the rung, the further the horizon. The entrance ministered abundantly at the
  top.
- **vv. 12–18** — the eyewitness claim, drawn as a place: the holy mount, the voice
  from the excellent glory, "we heard, when we were with him." Peter's own tabernacle
  (his body) noted as shortly to be put off.
- **vv. 19–21** — **a light bar that changes kind**: the more sure word of prophecy
  as "a light that shineth in a dark place, **until the day dawn, and the day star
  arise in your hearts**." Lamp, then dawn, then the star — three light sources in
  order, the lamp becoming unnecessary. Closing on prophecy coming not by the will of
  man, but holy men moved by the Holy Ghost.

**2 Peter 2 (22 vv.)** · ⏳ + 📊 — *three precedents, and who came out of each*

- **vv. 4–9** — a three-stop rail where the point is the **remainder** each time.
  Angels cast down to hell, delivered into chains of darkness — none spared. The old
  world flooded — **Noah the eighth person** saved, so eight, and the number is given.
  Sodom and Gomorrha turned into ashes — just Lot delivered, one. Draw the count that
  survives beneath each panel: 0, 8, 1. The conclusion is stated as a rule: the Lord
  knoweth how to deliver the godly and to reserve the unjust.
- **vv. 10–16** — the false teachers; then **Balaam**, "rebuked for his iniquity: the
  dumb ass speaking with man's voice forbad the madness of the prophet" — one figure
  drawn being corrected by the one below it in the order of creation.
- **vv. 17–19** — two promise-shaped things that deliver nothing: **wells without
  water** and **clouds carried with a tempest** — a well drawn dry, a cloud drawn
  passing. "While they promise them liberty, they themselves are the servants of
  corruption."
- **vv. 20–22** — the two proverbs as a **closed loop**: the dog turned to his own
  vomit again, the sow that was washed to her wallowing in the mire. Draw the path
  curving back to where it started, so the last frame is the first frame.

**2 Peter 3 (18 vv.)** · ⏳ + 🔢 — *three worlds, and two rulers laid together*

The best chapter in the NT short books, and the one to build first.

- **vv. 3–4** — the scoffers' claim, drawn as a flat unbroken line: "all things
  continue as they were from the beginning of the creation."
- **vv. 5–7** — the answer breaks that line into **three worlds with an element at
  each boundary**: the world that then was, being overflowed with **water**, perished;
  the heavens and the earth which are now, kept in store, reserved unto **fire**; and
  the word of God standing behind all three. A three-era bar with the two catastrophes
  as its dividers — and the flood era can borrow the already-built Genesis 6–9 water
  level directly.
- **v. 8** — **two rulers laid against each other**: "one day is with the Lord as a
  thousand years, and a thousand years as one day." Draw both scales on the same axis
  at their true relative sizes, and the scoffers' "where is the promise of his coming"
  answers itself visually. The single cleanest numeric idea in this file.
- **vv. 9–13** — the thief in the night; the heavens passing away with a great noise,
  the elements melting with fervent heat, the works burnt up — the second boundary
  crossing on the era bar. Then the third world drawn in: new heavens and a new earth,
  "wherein dwelleth righteousness."
- **vv. 14–18** — Paul's epistles noted as scripture, "in which are some things hard
  to be understood"; the warning against being led away with the error of the wicked;
  closing on growth in grace and knowledge — the ladder of chapter 1 given one more
  rung.

## Where to start

If these get built, the order that gets the most out of the least work:

1. **2 Peter 3** — three-world era bar plus the dual scale; reuses the Genesis 6–9
   flood level.
2. **Haggai 1–2** — the leaking bag and the contact-chain asymmetry are both complete
   graphics with no drawing debt, and the date rail ties the book together for free.
3. **Zephaniah 1** — inverts the already-built Genesis 1 chart; the cheapest strong
   idea in the file.
4. **2 John + 3 John as a diptych** — one threshold drawing, built once, used twice
   in opposite senses. Two whole books for the price of one.
5. **Habakkuk 3** — six empty stalls; the purest expression of the house style.
6. **Nahum 2** — the city cutaway with the river gates opening; the most cinematic
   single chapter here.
