# Short books (1–3 chapters) — per-chapter visual ideas

> **Status:** **2 Peter and Zephaniah are built** (2026-08-10) — 6 chapters,
> 114 verses, `generate_2peter_svg.py` and `generate_zephaniah_svg.py`, with
> the marks they share factored into `svg_vocab.py`. The other eleven books
> are unbuilt: 22 chapters, 405 verses, each chapter with a one-line entry in
> the [content catalog](../content-catalog.md) that points back here. Haggai
> 1–2 and the 2 John + 3 John diptych are the next builds (rated High there).
>
> Changes made while building 2 Peter: v19's liberty-and-bondage reversal
> needed its own frame rather than sharing one with the wells and clouds, and
> the chapter-2 precedents carry their survivor counts as numerals under the
> rail rather than inside the panels. For Zephaniah: 1:3's four categories are
> struck creature by creature rather than one specimen standing for each, and
> the four refusals of 3:2 are labelled in a row rather than radially, where
> the office panels left no room for their names.
>
> ⚠️ **Wording.** Quotations and names below are **WEB** (the project's only
> text, `passages.TRANSLATION`), checked against `web/bibles/WEB/`. Letter
> anything on a graphic from the WEB text itself, not from these notes.

Every book of the Bible with **three chapters or fewer**: 13 books, 28 chapters.
Six in the Old Testament, seven in the New.

Looked at for *maps and timelines*, these books read as low density. Read
instead for **contrast pairs, ordered sequences, ledgers, and thresholds**, and
most of them turn out to be well-suited — several are stronger per-chapter subjects
than the long books they sit next to, because a whole book fits in one world and the
camera never has to leave it.

Design rules assumed throughout (per the house style): the verse is being narrated,
so **no caption lines and no prose on the graphic** — names, numbers and labelled
drawings only. One world per chapter, revealed by a per-verse camera.

Type icons follow the [content catalog](../content-catalog.md#legend):
🗺️ map · ⏳ timeline · 🌳 genealogy · 🏛️ architectural · 🔄 process/cycle ·
📊 comparison · 🔢 numeric · 💡 symbolic

## Old Testament

### Obadiah — 1 chapter

**Obadiah (21 vv.)** · 💡 + 🗺️ — *one cliff, drawn in section*

The chapter has a single governing axis: **height**. Edom lives "in the clefts of
the rock," sets its nest "among the stars," and every judgment line is a descent.
Draw one cliff face in cross-section, Edom's eyrie near the top, and let the camera
fall down it as the oracle proceeds.

- **vv. 1–4** — the nest among the stars, and the line that brings it down. An
  altitude gauge on the margin is the whole progress indicator; no counter needed.
- **vv. 5–7** — the gleaning logic: thieves leave something, grape pickers leave
  something, Esau is left nothing. Three small harvest bins, two with a remainder,
  the third scraped clean. The confederate allies then drawn on the wrong side of
  the border line.
- **vv. 8–9** — the wise men of Edom and the mighty men of Teman struck off by name.
- **vv. 10–14** — **the crossroads (v. 14) is the set-piece.** Eight successive
  "Don't…" clauses, each a specific act at a specific moment: looking down on your
  brother, rejoicing, speaking proudly, entering the gate, seizing their wealth,
  standing in the crossroads "to cut off those of his who escape". Draw a literal
  fork in the road with Edom
  posted at it and the fugitive lines running through — each verse marks one more
  refused exit. This is the emotional centre and it is genuinely diagrammatic.
- **vv. 15–18** — the cup passed round the nations; Jacob fire, Joseph flame, Esau
  stubble — three labelled materials, one outcome.
- **vv. 19–21** — the chapter turns into an actual **map**: the South possesses
  the mountain of Esau, the lowland the Philistines, then the fields of Ephraim and
  Samaria; Benjamin takes Gilead; the captives reach to Zarephath, and those in
  Sepharad possess the cities of the Negev. The cliff view widens out to a territory
  diagram, saviors going up on Mount Zion opposite the emptied crag.

### Haggai — 2 chapters

The most date-stamped short book in the canon — five oracles, each pinned to a day
of Darius' second year. **A date rail runs the length of both chapters** and is the
book's spine; the two chapters simply place different drawings above it.

**Haggai 1 (15 vv.)** · 📊 + 🏛️ — *two houses and a bag with a hole*

- **v. 1** — first pin on the rail: sixth month, first day.
- **vv. 2–4** — two buildings side by side at the same scale: a paneled private
  house, finished; Yahweh's house, a bare foundation. This pairing holds
  the whole chapter.
- **vv. 5–6** — **the futility ledger, the best single graphic in Haggai 1.** Five
  rows, each an input that fails to arrive at its output: sown much / brought in
  little; eat / not enough; drink / not filled; clothe / not warm; earn wages / a
  bag with holes. Draw the bag with the hole and let the coins fall through. Five
  rows, no sentences.
- **vv. 7–11** — the timber route up the mountain; the drought called down on the
  grain, new wine, oil, men, livestock, and "all the labor of the hands" — the same
  ledger rows, now struck out at source.
- **vv. 12–15** — Zerubbabel, Joshua the high priest and the remnant obey; second
  pin on the rail, the 24th day, 23 days after the first. The foundation begins to
  carry courses.

**Haggai 2 (23 vv.)** · 💡 + 🔄 — *the ghosted first temple, and a contact chain*

- **vv. 1–9** — seventh month, 21st day. "Who is left among you who saw this house
  in its former glory?" Draw Solomon's temple **ghosted behind the new work, at true
  relative scale**, so the shortfall is seen rather than stated; then the shaking of
  heavens, earth, sea and dry land, and the silver and gold marked as already owned.
  The latter house's outline finally overtops the ghost.
- **vv. 10–14** — ninth month, 24th day. **The priests' two rulings are a real
  asymmetry diagram, and the standout idea in the book.** Ruling one: holy meat
  carried in the fold of a garment touching bread, stew, wine, oil — nothing is made
  holy. Ruling two: one unclean by reason of a dead body touches any of the same —
  all are unclean.
  Draw one contact chain twice: holiness stops dead after the first link, uncleanness
  propagates down every link. Two rows of the same four objects, one row staying
  plain, the other going dark all the way along.
- **vv. 15–19** — the shortfall numbers: a heap of twenty measures yielding ten, a
  wine vat expected to draw fifty yielding twenty. Two measure-jars each drawn
  twice, actual against expected; blight, mildew and hail marked on the rows. Then
  the pivot — "From today I will bless you" — and the jars fill.
- **vv. 20–23** — same day, second word: the throne of kingdoms overthrown, chariots
  and horses down, everyone by the sword of his brother; closing on the signet ring,
  drawn small and alone after the wreckage.

### Joel — 3 chapters

One landscape serves all three chapters: a field, a city on a rise behind it, a
valley below. Chapter 1 strips it, chapter 2 assaults and then restores it, chapter
3 empties the nations into the valley.

**Joel 1 (20 vv.)** · 🔄 + 🔢 — *four passes over one field*

- **v. 4** — the four-stage chain: swarming locust → great locust → grasshopper →
  caterpillar, each eating what the last one left. Four passes across the same field,
  the ground a stage barer each time. This is the chapter's clock.
- **vv. 5–12** — the produce ledger going out crop by crop as it is named: vine,
  fig tree (barked white), pomegranate, palm, apple, wheat, barley. Each named crop
  is one row that empties on its own verse.
- **vv. 9–13** — the temple with the meal offering and the drink offering visibly
  cut off; the priests in sackcloth.
- **vv. 14–20** — the solemn assembly called; the seeds rotting under their clods,
  the granaries laid desolate, the barns broken down, the herds perplexed for want
  of pasture, the water brooks dried up. The landscape's last colours drain out.

**Joel 2 (32 vv.)** · 💡 + 🔄 — *the swarm becomes an army, then the ledger refills*

- **vv. 1–11** — the trumpet in Zion; the same swarm redrawn **in ranks** — running as
  horsemen, leaping on the tops of the mountains, not breaking ranks, not jostling one
  another, climbing the wall, entering in at the windows like thieves. Draw the city
  wall in elevation and the column going up it in formation. Sun, moon and stars
  withdraw above.
- **vv. 12–17** — the turn. "Tear your heart, and not your garments." The trumpet
  sounds a second time and now it **gathers** instead of alarms: a summons strip of
  who is called, in the text's own order — elders, children, those who nurse from
  breasts, the bridegroom out of his room, the bride out of her room, the priests
  between the porch and the altar. Six figures taking their places.
- **vv. 18–27** — **restoration read directly off chapter 1's ledger.** The rain
  comes, the threshing floors fill with wheat, the vats overflow with new wine and
  oil — and "I will restore to you the years that the swarming locust has eaten"
  (with the great locust, the grasshopper and the caterpillar) refills the four bars
  the four insects emptied, in reverse order of the eating. The single best payoff
  in the book, and it costs nothing because the ledger is already drawn.
- **vv. 28–32** — the Spirit poured out: sons and daughters prophesy, old men dream,
  young men see visions, servants and handmaids included — four labelled groups
  under one downpour. Blood, fire and pillars of smoke below; sun to darkness and
  moon to blood above; the escape on Zion marked.

**Joel 3 (21 vv.)** · 🗺️ + 📊 — *the valley of decision*

- **vv. 1–8** — the valley of Jehoshaphat as the assembly floor. Around it, a trade
  ring: Tyre, Sidon, all the regions of Philistia, the Greeks, the men of Sheba, with
  arrows showing where the children were sold and what for (a boy for a prostitute,
  a girl for wine). Then **every arrow reverses** — sold into the hands of the
  children of Judah.
  A traffic diagram run backwards.
- **vv. 9–15** — "Beat your plowshares into swords, and your pruning hooks into
  spears": two tools drawn mid-conversion, the famous line inverted. The weak saying
  "I am strong." Multitudes funnelling into the valley of decision; the sickle for
  the ripe harvest and the full wine press; sun, moon and stars dark again.
- **vv. 16–21** — the reversal completes: the mountains drop down sweet wine, the
  hills flow with milk, the brooks of Judah flow with waters, and a fountain flows
  out from Yahweh's house to water the valley of Shittim. Egypt and Edom drawn as the
  two dry patches left on an otherwise watered map.

### Nahum — 3 chapters

One city, approached, entered, and compared to a city that already fell. The three
chapters are a natural approach → assault → verdict progression.

**Nahum 1 (15 vv.)** · 💡 + 📊 — *a storm front, and one rock in it*

- **vv. 1–6** — the theophany as weather: whirlwind and storm, the clouds the dust
  of his feet. Bashan, Carmel and Lebanon drawn as three named ridges that wither in
  turn as the front crosses them — the landscape is the progress bar.
- **vv. 7–11** — the chapter's hinge, and a clean two-column ledger: "a stronghold in
  the day of trouble; and he knows those who take refuge in him" against "with an
  overflowing flood, he will make a full end of her place." One rock standing in
  rising water, people on it; everything else level with the flood.
- **vv. 12–15** — the yoke broken and the bonds burst; the engraved image and the
  molten image cut off out of the house of the gods. Closing on **the feet on the
  mountains** of him who brings good news — arriving from the same direction the
  storm came.
- *(Optional structure note: vv. 2–8 are a partial Hebrew alphabetic acrostic. If the
  Psalm 119 / Lamentations acrostic treatment ever gets built, the letter ticks could
  be reused here as a quiet margin device.)*

**Nahum 2 (13 vv.)** · 🏛️ + 🔄 — *the city in cutaway, taken piece by piece*

The most cinematic chapter in the twelve minor prophets, and it maps onto a single
architectural cutaway: wall, river gates, palace, treasury, and the lions' den behind.

- **vv. 1–5** — the defence mounted: keep the fortress, watch the way, strengthen
  your waist. Then the attackers — the shield made red, the valiant men in scarlet,
  the chariots raging in the streets and rushing back and forth in the wide ways,
  like torches, running like the lightnings.
  Draw the streets in plan and let the chariot tracks scribble across them.
- **vv. 6–7** — **"The gates of the rivers are opened, and the palace is
  dissolved."** The river gate opens on its own verse and the water takes the palace
  section of the drawing. She is carried away, her servants moaning as with the
  voice of doves.
- **vv. 8–10** — "Nineveh has been from of old like a pool of water, yet they flee
  away": the pool draining. Then the spoil ledger — silver, gold, "there is no end of
  the store" — counted up and then emptied to nothing across three words: empty,
  void, and waste.
- **vv. 11–13** — the closing image needs no text at all: **the lions' den, drawn
  empty.** The lion, the lioness, the lion's cubs, the feeding place of the young
  lions — all labelled, all vacant, the torn prey gone.

**Nahum 3 (19 vv.)** · 📊 — *Nineveh set beside No-Amon (Thebes)*

- **vv. 1–7** — the bloody city: the horseman mounting, the flashing sword and the
  glittering spear, the heap of corpses. The alluring prostitute, the mistress of
  witchcraft, selling nations and families; the shame spectacle.
- **vv. 8–10** — **the spine of the chapter: "Are you better than No-Amon?"** Two city
  profiles side by side, each with the identical row of defences and allies listed
  beneath — situated among the rivers, the waters around her, the sea her rampart,
  Cush and Egypt her strength, Put and Libya her helpers. Thebes' rows are already
  struck through; Nineveh's strike through one at a time as the chapter proceeds. A
  direct, honest comparison chart the text itself sets up.
- **vv. 11–15** — the fortresses as fig trees with the first-ripe figs: shaken, they
  fall into the mouth of the eater. The gates set wide open, the bars devoured by
  fire. "Draw water for the siege. Strengthen your fortresses… Make the brick kiln
  strong" — preparations drawn while the wall behind is already breached.
- **vv. 16–19** — the guards and the officials as locusts settling on the walls on a
  cold day; when the sun appears they **flee away, and their place is not known**.
  The swarm that opened Joel closes Nahum, departing. The wound with no healing; all
  that hear the report clapping.

### Habakkuk — 3 chapters

A dialogue, a watchtower, and a march. Three completely different geometries — the
best per-chapter variety of any short book.

**Habakkuk 1 (17 vv.)** · 💡 📊 — *a two-sided exchange*

Use the **speaker-strip-with-arcs device already built for Genesis 3**: every line
drawn as an arc from speaker to hearer, arcs persisting so the exchange reads whole.
Here there are only two speakers and four moves.

- **vv. 2–4** — first complaint: how long? Destruction and violence, the law
  paralyzed, justice coming out perverted, the wicked surrounding the righteous.
  Draw the perverted justice as a bent line where a straight one is expected.
- **vv. 5–11** — the answer, and a nasty little attribute chart: the Chaldeans'
  horses swifter than leopards, more fierce than the evening wolves, flying as an
  eagle that hurries to devour. Three animals labelled with the one quality each
  contributes. They gather prisoners like sand, scoff at kings, build up an earthen
  ramp and take every stronghold.
- **vv. 12–17** — second complaint, and the image that carries it: **men made like the
  fish of the sea, taken up with the hook, gathered in the dragnet** — and the fisher
  then sacrificing to his net and burning incense to his dragnet. Draw the net
  hauling, and an altar with a net on it. The chapter ends unanswered, on the hook.

**Habakkuk 2 (20 vv.)** · 🏛️ + 📊 — *the tower, the tablet, five woes cut into it*

- **vv. 1–4** — the watchtower, drawn as the vantage the whole chapter is seen from.
  "**Write the vision, and make it plain on tablets, that he who runs may read
  it**" — the book asks for an infographic in so many words; the tablet is the surface
  everything else in the chapter is inscribed on.
- **vv. 5–19** — **five woes, five stanzas, five emblems cut into the tablet**, one
  per turn of the camera:
  1. him who increases that which is not his, and enriches himself by extortion — a
     stack of what he has taken, his debtors rising up suddenly;
  2. him who gets an evil gain for his house, that he may set his nest on high — the
     nest again, answering Obadiah;
  3. him who builds a town with blood — a foundation course laid in red, with
     **the stone crying out of the wall and the beam out of the woodwork answering
     it** (v. 11), drawn literally as two marked members of the same building;
  4. him who gives his neighbor drink — the cup, and the cup of Yahweh's right hand
     coming around to him;
  5. him who says to the wood, "Awake!" — the mute stone overlaid with gold and
     silver, with no breath at all within it.
- **v. 14** — between the woes, the widening flood: "the earth will be filled with
  the knowledge of Yahweh's glory, **as the waters cover the sea**." The one
  bright interruption; let it wash across the tablet.
- **v. 20** — everything goes quiet: the holy temple, and every figure in the chapter
  silent before it.

**Habakkuk 3 (19 vv.)** · 🗺️ + 🔢 — *a march, and six empty stalls*

- **vv. 1–2** — the psalm heading ("set to victorious music", and the closing "For the
  music director, on my stringed instruments") frames the chapter as sung; a plain
  staff or string motif at the margin is enough to mark it.
- **vv. 3–15** — a **route across named ground**: God from Teman, the Holy One from
  Mount Paran; rays shining from his hand, plague before him and pestilence at his
  feet; the ancient mountains crumbled, the tents of Cushan in affliction, the
  dwellings of the land of Midian trembling; the rivers, the sea, and the sun and
  moon standing still in the sky. Draw the named places as a march line, with each
  element of the landscape reacting on its own verse.
- **vv. 17–19** — **the ending is the best "less is more" graphic in the book.** Six
  labelled rows, every one at zero: the fig tree not flourishing, no fruit in the
  vines, the labor of the olive failing, the fields yielding no food, the flocks cut
  off from the fold, no herd in the stalls. Draw six empty stalls and bins, all
  counted, all nothing — then one figure with feet like deer's feet, up on the high
  places above them. No words needed beyond the six labels.

### Zephaniah — 3 chapters ✅ Built

**Zephaniah 1 (18 vv.)** · 💡 + 🗺️ — *creation run backwards, then a lantern through the city*

- **vv. 2–3** — **the sweep list runs Genesis 1's creation order in reverse**: man,
  animal, the birds of the sky, the fish of the sea. The Genesis 1 graphic is already
  built as a forming-then-filling chart — **invert it.** Four bands emptying from the
  top down, in the exact reverse of the order they were filled. The strongest
  cross-book idea in this whole file.
- **vv. 4–6** — the targets named: the remnant of Baal, the idolatrous and pagan
  priests, those who worship the army of the sky on the housetops, those who swear by
  Yahweh and also by Malcam, those who have turned back. Rooftops drawn along the
  skyline, each marked.
- **vv. 7–13** — the day of Yahweh as a **sacrifice with guests consecrated**, and
  then a search of the city by quarter: the fish gate, the second quarter, the hills,
  Maktesh, the people of Canaan. Draw Jerusalem in plan and move a lantern through it
  — "I will search Jerusalem with lamps" — quarter by quarter, finding the men settled
  on their dregs. Houses built but not inhabited, vineyards planted but not drunk of:
  two more empty-ledger rows.
- **vv. 14–18** — the word ladder that became the *Dies Irae*: the nine words of 1:15
  — wrath, distress, anguish, trouble, ruin, darkness, gloom, clouds, blackness — then
  the trumpet and alarm of v. 16. Each step darker than the last — a bar that goes to
  black one word at a time. Silver and gold explicitly unable to deliver.

**Zephaniah 2 (15 vv.)** · 🗺️ — *a compass laid over Judah*

The cleanest map chapter in the short books, because the oracles go round the points
of the compass in order.

- **vv. 1–3** — the humble of the land gathered at the centre before the circuit starts.
- **vv. 4–7** — **west:** the four Philistine cities in a row — Gaza forsaken,
  Ashkelon a desolation, Ashdod driven out at noonday, Ekron rooted up. The
  sea coast then redrawn as pastures with cottages for shepherds and folds for
  flocks, for the remnant of Judah.
- **vv. 8–11** — **east:** Moab as Sodom, Ammon as Gomorrah — a possession of nettles,
  and salt pits, and a perpetual desolation. Two ruined sites, labelled with their
  precedents.
- **v. 12** — **south:** the Cushites, one line, one mark.
- **vv. 13–15** — **north:** Assyria, and Nineveh made a desolation, as dry as the
  wilderness. Herds lying down in the middle of her, the pelican and the porcupine
  lodging in its capitals, their calls echoing through the windows, desolation in
  the thresholds, the cedar beams laid bare. Draw the ruin with the creatures
  occupying the named architectural parts — capitals, windows, thresholds, cedar
  beams — a building labelled by its squatters.

**Zephaniah 3 (20 vv.)** · 📊 + 💡 — *four offices, failed and refilled*

- **vv. 1–7** — the four offices of the city, each with its own image: her princes
  roaring lions, her judges evening wolves, her prophets arrogant and treacherous,
  her priests profaning the sanctuary. Four panels, four emblems, all four struck.
  Set against them, at the centre, "Yahweh, within her, is righteous… Every morning
  he brings his justice to light. He doesn't fail" — a morning tick that keeps
  arriving on schedule while the four fail.
- **vv. 8–13** — the turn: the lips of the peoples purified, worshipers coming from
  beyond the rivers of Cush bringing an offering — arrows now running **inward** on the
  compass map of chapter 2, from the same points that were struck. The afflicted and
  poor remnant feeding and lying down with none making them afraid.
- **vv. 14–20** — the four panels refill, and the phrase "among you" comes back for
  the last time (3:15, 3:17) — this time the centre figure is not judging but
  **singing**: "He will calm you in his love. He will rejoice over you with
  singing." The gathering closes it: the lame saved, those driven away gathered,
  their fortunes restored before their eyes.

## New Testament

### Philemon — 1 chapter

**Philemon (25 vv.)** · 💡 + 🔢 + 🗺️ — *three people, one route, one ledger*

Three infographic subjects, and they fit on one page.

- **vv. 1–7** — the cast, drawn as a small relationship diagram rather than a list:
  Paul (a prisoner — draw the chain, it is referenced four times), Timothy, Philemon,
  Apphia, Archippus, and **the assembly in your house** — so the house is the frame
  the whole chapter sits inside.
- **vv. 8–16** — **the status change is the graphic.** One figure, Onesimus, with the
  label on him changing on its own verse: *useless* → *useful* (the name itself
  means useful — the pun is the argument), then *slave* → *more than a slave, a
  beloved brother*. The arrow between Onesimus and Philemon is redrawn each time.
  Beneath it, the round trip: Colossae → Rome (a runaway, going one way) → Colossae
  (carrying this letter, going back).
- **vv. 17–19** — **the ledger, and the best single idea in the chapter.** "If he
  has wronged you at all, or owes you anything, put that to my account. I, Paul,
  write this with my own hand: I will repay it." Draw a two-column account with the
  debt transferred across from Onesimus to Paul, in Paul's own hand. Then the counter-
  entry that swallows it: "not to mention to you that you owe to me even your own
  self besides" — an unbilled line beneath, larger than the debt above it.
- **vv. 20–25** — the guest room prepared, and the greeting list: Epaphras, Mark,
  Aristarchus, Demas, Luke. Five names round the house that opened the page.

### 2 John — 1 chapter

**2 John (13 vv.)** · 💡 — *a threshold, rightly closed*

The whole letter is about **what crosses a doorway.** Draw the chosen lady's house
with one door and put everything at it.

- **vv. 1–6** — truth and love arriving together and going in: "not as though I wrote
  to you a new commandment, but that which we had from the beginning" — an arrow
  running back down the timeline rather than forward, which is the letter's one
  temporal move.
- **vv. 7–9** — the deceivers, "those who don't confess that Jesus Christ came in
  the flesh", approaching the door. "Watch yourselves, that we don't lose the things
  which we have accomplished" — the things already accomplished drawn inside, at risk.
- **vv. 10–11** — the door shut: don't receive him into your house, and don't
  welcome him. This is the image the letter exists for; hold the camera on it.
- **vv. 12–13** — paper and ink set aside for **face to face** — the closing emblem,
  shared with 3 John (see below).

### 3 John — 1 chapter

**3 John (14 vv.)** · 📊 + 💡 — *three doorways, and one wrongly bolted*

**Design 2 and 3 John as a deliberate diptych** — the same threshold drawn twice, in
opposite senses. It is the cheapest pairing in the file and the most satisfying.

- Three doorways side by side, one per named man, each resolving on its own verses:
  - **Gaius (vv. 1–8)** — open. He does a faithful work for the brothers and
    strangers, and sends them forward on their journey. Draw the travelling brothers
    "taking nothing from the Gentiles," so the supply line has to come from inside the
    door — a small supply arrow that only works if the door stands open.
  - **Diotrephes (vv. 9–11)** — bolted, and this is the wrong one. He "loves to be
    first among them," doesn't accept what we say, accuses us unjustly with wicked
    words, does not himself receive the brothers, **forbids those who would**, and
    throws them out of the assembly. Two arrows blocked and one person thrown back
    through the door.
  - **Demetrius (v. 12)** — a clean report over the lintel: "the testimony of all,
    and of the truth itself."
- **vv. 13–14** — the same ending as 2 John: ink and pen laid down, face to face,
  "The friends greet you. Greet the friends by name." Draw the two letters' endings
  identically so the pair closes together.

### Jude — 1 chapter

**Jude (25 vv.)** · 📊 + 💡 + 🌳 — *triads, and six things failing at their own job*

Densest single chapter in the file. Jude thinks in threes, so the layout can too.

- **vv. 3–4** — contending earnestly for the faith; certain men who crept in
  secretly.
- **vv. 5–7** — **three judgment precedents, one rail**: the people saved out of the
  land of Egypt then destroyed; the angels who didn't keep their first domain, kept
  in everlasting bonds under darkness; Sodom and Gomorrah shown as an example.
  Three panels in time order.
- **vv. 8–11** — Michael contending with the devil about the body of Moses (drawn as
  a disputed object between two figures, and nothing more); then **the second triad**:
  the way of Cain, the error of Balaam, Korah's rebellion — three routes leading off
  one road, each labelled with its man.
- **vv. 12–13** — **the set-piece, and the purest "labelled drawings, no prose"
  passage in the whole file.** Six things drawn failing at the one thing they exist to
  do:
  1. clouds without water, carried along by winds — no rain falls from them;
  2. autumn trees without fruit, twice dead, plucked up by the roots — drawn
     upended, roots in the air;
  3. wild waves of the sea, foaming out their own shame — motion that produces only
     foam;
  4. wandering stars — a star with no fixed course, drifting off the chart into the
     blackness of darkness reserved for it.
  (Six images across four clauses; give each its own beat of camera.)
- **vv. 14–16** — **Enoch, "the seventh from Adam"** — a generation count, so pull
  the seven nodes straight out of the already-built Genesis 5 graphic and light the
  seventh. A free cross-book link, and it makes the prophecy's antiquity visible
  instead of stated.
- **vv. 17–23** — the four instructions (build up, pray, keep, look) and then a
  **three-way triage**, which is a genuine process diagram: "On some have compassion,
  making a distinction, and some save, snatching them out of the fire with fear,
  hating even the clothing stained by the flesh." Three cases, three different
  responses, one of them handled at arm's length.
- **vv. 24–25** — closing on "him who is able to keep them from stumbling" — every
  falling thing in the chapter (angels, stars, trees, men) answered by one that
  keeps from falling.

### Titus — 3 chapters

**Titus 1 (16 vv.)** · 🗺️ + 📊 — *an island, city by city*

- **vv. 1–5** — Crete drawn as a real island map: "that you would set in order the
  things that were lacking, and **appoint elders in every city**." City dots along
  the coast, each waiting for a marker, each filled as the chapter proceeds.
- **vv. 6–9** — the qualification list as a two-sided checklist against one figure: on
  one side the disqualifiers (self-pleasing, easily angered, given to wine, violent,
  greedy for dishonest gain), on the other the requirements (blameless, the husband of
  one wife, children who believe, given to hospitality, a lover of good, sober minded,
  fair, holy, self-controlled, holding to the faithful word). Names and words only, no
  sentences.
- **vv. 10–16** — the counterweight: many unruly men, vain talkers, "whose mouths
  must be stopped; men who overthrow whole houses" — draw the houses being overthrown
  against the cities being set in order, the same island read two ways. The Cretan
  proverb quoted by a prophet of their own; "To the pure, all things are pure" as the
  closing test.

**Titus 2 (15 vv.)** · 📊 + ⏳ — *the household table, and two appearings*

- **vv. 1–10** — **a five-row table, and it is already a table in the text.** Older
  men, older women, young wives, younger men, servants — each row carrying its charge
  and, crucially, its **purpose clause**, which the other household codes don't spell
  out this consistently: *that God's word may not be blasphemed*, *that he who
  opposes you may be ashamed*, *that they may adorn the doctrine of God, our Savior,
  in all things*. Five groups, five charges, three reasons — one clean grid.
- **vv. 11–15** — **the two appearings**, drawn as a bracket: the grace of God that
  *has appeared* behind, the appearing of the glory ahead, and "this present age"
  between them, with the three adverbs — soberly, righteously, godly — placed inside
  the span. A timeline only three marks long, which is exactly the right size.

**Titus 3 (15 vv.)** · 📊 + 🔄 — *were / now, with a washing at the pivot*

- **vv. 1–2** — the civic row: in subjection to rulers and authorities, ready for
  every good work, speaking evil of no one, not contentious, gentle, showing all
  humility toward all men.
- **vv. 3–7** — **the before/after, hinged on one word.** Left column, "we were also
  once": foolish, disobedient, deceived, serving various lusts and pleasures, living
  in malice and envy, hateful, hating one another. Then *But when the kindness of
  God our Savior and his love toward mankind appeared* — the pivot, drawn as the
  washing of regeneration, "not by works of righteousness which we did ourselves."
  Right column
  fills: justified by his grace, heirs according to the hope of eternal life. Two
  columns, one basin between them.
- **vv. 8–11** — what to avoid, listed flat: foolish questionings, genealogies,
  strife, and disputes about the law. Then a small **two-strike process diagram**:
  a first warning, a second warning, avoid. Three steps, drawn as three.
- **vv. 12–15** — the logistics coda: Artemas or Tychicus sent, winter at Nicopolis,
  Zenas the lawyer and Apollos brought on their journey. A short route strip and four
  names — the island map from chapter 1, zoomed out one notch.

### 2 Thessalonians — 3 chapters

**2 Thessalonians 1 (12 vv.)** · 📊 — *a balance that tips at the revealing*

- **vv. 3–4** — two rising bars: faith growing exceedingly, love abounding. Drawn
  small, at the top, as the measured facts of the report.
- **vv. 5–10** — **the recompense ledger, and it is literally a balance.** On one
  pan, those who afflict you; on the other, you who are afflicted. At the moment of
  the revealing — the Lord from heaven with his mighty angels, in flaming fire — the
  loads **swap sides**: affliction repaid to those who afflict, relief to the
  afflicted. Draw one
  beam, two pans, and let the verse tip it.
- **vv. 11–12** — the counting worthy, and the name glorified in his saints; the fire
  from v. 8 resolving into the glory of v. 10 rather than being a second image.

**2 Thessalonians 2 (17 vv.)** · ⏳ + 🔄 — *a gated sequence with one door held shut*

The strongest diagram in the short-book NT: a strict ordering the text insists on.

- **vv. 1–3** — "**it will not be, unless the rebellion comes first**" — the whole
  chapter is an ordering claim, so draw it as gates on a rail that cannot
  open out of turn.
- **vv. 3–7** — the gates in order: the rebellion → **the one who restrains now,
  until he is taken out of the way** (draw the restrainer as a bar across the door,
  the one element holding the sequence still) → the man of sin revealed, sitting as
  God in the temple of God, setting himself up as God.
- **vv. 8–12** — the last gate opens and closes at once: killed with the breath of
  his mouth, destroyed by the manifestation of his coming. Beside it, the false
  apparatus — power and signs and lying wonders, a working of error, believing a
  lie —
  drawn as a counterfeit of the real signs elsewhere in the rail.
- **vv. 13–17** — the counter-sequence for the readers, running the other way from the
  beginning: chosen from the beginning, through sanctification of the Spirit and
  belief in the truth, called through the Good News, for the obtaining of the glory.
  Two rails, opposite directions.

**2 Thessalonians 3 (18 vv.)** · 🔄 + 💡 — *two walks, and a signature*

- **The whole-chapter bracket:** the letter opens by warning against a "letter as if
  from us" (2:2) and closes with "**The greeting of me, Paul, with my own hand, which
  is the sign in every letter: this is how I write**" (3:17). A forged letter at one
  end, a verified hand at the other — draw the seal/signature as the closing emblem
  and it answers something set up two chapters earlier.
- **vv. 6–12** — two walking tracks side by side. The ordered walk: the tradition
  received, and Paul's own night-and-day labor, eating no one's bread without paying
  for it. The rebellious walk: not working at all, but busybodies. Between them the
  rule, stated as a rule and drawn as a gate — "**If anyone will not work, don't let
  him eat**."
- **vv. 13–15** — the discipline drawn as **distance, not expulsion**: note that man
  and have no company with him — "Don't count him as an enemy, but admonish him as
  a brother." He moves further out on the diagram; he does not leave it. Worth being
  precise about, because most treatments get it wrong.

### 2 Peter — 3 chapters ✅ Built

**2 Peter 1 (21 vv.)** · 📊 + 💡 — *a ladder, and how far it lets you see*

- **vv. 5–7** — **the ladder**, already flagged in the catalog: faith → moral
  excellence → knowledge → self-control → perseverance → godliness → brotherly
  affection → love. Eight
  rungs, each added on its own clause. The chapter's spine.
- **vv. 8–11** — the payoff drawn as **range of sight**: he who lacks these things is
  blind, "seeing only what is near." Put a sight-line gauge beside the ladder — the
  higher the rung, the further the horizon. The entrance, richly supplied, at the
  top.
- **vv. 12–18** — the eyewitness claim, drawn as a place: the holy mountain, the voice
  from the Majestic Glory, "We heard this voice come out of heaven when we were with
  him." Peter's own tent (his body) noted as shortly to be put off.
- **vv. 19–21** — **a light bar that changes kind**: the more sure word of prophecy
  "as to a lamp shining in a dark place, **until the day dawns, and the morning star
  arises in your hearts**." Lamp, then dawn, then the star — three light sources in
  order, the lamp becoming unnecessary. Closing on prophecy coming not by the will of
  man, but holy men of God moved by the Holy Spirit.

**2 Peter 2 (22 vv.)** · ⏳ + 📊 — *three precedents, and who came out of each*

- **vv. 4–9** — a three-stop rail where the point is the **remainder** each time.
  Angels cast down to Tartarus, committed to pits of darkness — none spared. The
  ancient world flooded — **Noah with seven others** preserved, so eight, and the
  number is given. Sodom and Gomorrah turned into ashes — righteous Lot delivered,
  one. Draw the count that survives beneath each panel: 0, 8, 1. The conclusion is
  stated as a rule: the Lord knows how to deliver the godly and to keep the
  unrighteous under punishment.
- **vv. 10–16** — the false teachers; then **Balaam the son of Beor**, "rebuked for
  his own disobedience. A mute donkey spoke with a man's voice and stopped the
  madness of the prophet" — one figure
  drawn being corrected by the one below it in the order of creation.
- **vv. 17–19** — two promise-shaped things that deliver nothing: **wells without
  water** and **clouds driven by a storm** — a well drawn dry, a cloud drawn
  passing. "promising them liberty, while they themselves are bondservants of
  corruption."
- **vv. 20–22** — the two proverbs as a **closed loop**: "The dog turns to his own
  vomit again," and "the sow that has washed to wallowing in the mire." Draw the path
  curving back to where it started, so the last frame is the first frame.

**2 Peter 3 (18 vv.)** · ⏳ + 🔢 — *three worlds, and two rulers laid together*

The best chapter in the NT short books, and the one to build first.

- **vv. 3–4** — the mockers' claim, drawn as a flat unbroken line: "all things
  continue as they were from the beginning of the creation."
- **vv. 5–7** — the answer breaks that line into **three worlds with an element at
  each boundary**: the world that then was, being overflowed with **water**, perished;
  the heavens that now exist, and the earth, stored up for **fire**; and
  the word of God standing behind all three. A three-era bar with the two catastrophes
  as its dividers — and the flood era can borrow the already-built Genesis 6–9 water
  level directly.
- **v. 8** — **two rulers laid against each other**: "one day is with the Lord as a
  thousand years, and a thousand years as one day." Draw both scales on the same axis
  at their true relative sizes, and the mockers' "Where is the promise of his coming?"
  answers itself visually. The single cleanest numeric idea in this file.
- **vv. 9–13** — the thief in the night; the heavens passing away with a great noise,
  the elements dissolved with fervent heat, the works burned up — the second boundary
  crossing on the era bar. Then the third world drawn in: new heavens and a new earth,
  "in which righteousness dwells."
- **vv. 14–18** — Paul's letters set beside "the other Scriptures", in which "there
  are some things that are hard to understand"; the warning against being carried
  away with the error of the wicked;
  closing on growth in grace and knowledge — the ladder of chapter 1 given one more
  rung.

## Where to start

The order that gets the most out of the least work. Items 1 and 3 are built, so
Haggai 1–2 and the 2 John + 3 John diptych are next.

1. **2 Peter 3** ✅ built — three-world era bar plus the dual scale; reuses the
   Genesis 6–9 flood level.
2. **Haggai 1–2** — the leaking bag and the contact-chain asymmetry are both complete
   graphics with no drawing debt, and the date rail ties the book together for free.
3. **Zephaniah 1** ✅ built — inverts the already-built Genesis 1 chart; the cheapest
   strong idea in the file.
4. **2 John + 3 John as a diptych** — one threshold drawing, built once, used twice
   in opposite senses. Two whole books for the price of one.
5. **Habakkuk 3** — six empty stalls; the purest expression of the house style.
6. **Nahum 2** — the city cutaway with the river gates opening; the most cinematic
   single chapter here.
