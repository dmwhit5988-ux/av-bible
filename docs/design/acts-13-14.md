# Acts 13–14: the camera tour and the scene cards

Paul's first journey as one continuous camera flight over a large map, the
way Numbers 34 is built, with the long stops (the sermon, the two Sabbaths,
Lystra) told as Israel's story flown over the map and as side-view scene
cards the camera dives into from a town's dot.

What it shows:

- **The journey** (13:1–14, 13:51–14:7, 14:20–28) on the map: the road ahead
  dashed, each leg tracing in gold on its verse, sea legs dotted, the road home
  (14:21–26) a double line beside the road out. A plate names the prophets and
  teachers (13:1–3) and the proconsul and the sorcerer (13:7–12). At 13:13 the
  camera pulls back far enough to show John's own road home to Jerusalem.
- **Israel's story over the map** (13:17–26): the fathers at Hebron, the people
  (a band of twelve dots) going down to Egypt and out of it, the wilderness with
  "40 years", seven towers falling, the tribal lands filling as the people
  reach them with "450 years", the judges' sparks, Samuel, Saul in Benjamin,
  David at Bethlehem with the manger light, John at the Jordan and the Chi-Rho
  coming down the valley to him. At 13:26 the camera rises to the whole eastern
  sea and the word runs from Jerusalem to Antioch while a glint runs along the
  journey to Antioch of Pisidia.
- **The synagogue card** (13:15–16, 13:38–50). The camera comes down into the
  hall at 13:15: the scroll is read, and the rulers invite Paul and Barnabas.
  At 13:16 Paul stands and the two benches are pulsed in turn. The camera leaves
  for the story at 13:17 and comes back at 13:38: the benches, the law of Moses
  in the ark, the prophets on the desk, the Chi-Rho rising over the desk at
  13:41.
  The two Sabbaths are acted out:
  - the hall empties and the followers trail the two out;
  - almost the whole city fills the plaza;
  - the objectors gather on the steps, told apart by place and posture alone;
  - the Chi-Rho goes out of the synagogue to Paul and Barnabas and the door
    shuts behind it;
  - at 13:47 ("a light for the Gentiles") the camera rises to the whole sea
    while the Chi-Rho keeps its size and rings run from it to every edge, then
    comes back down;
  - the crowd lifts its arms, and the believers walk out of the gate into the
    hills;
  - at 13:50 the two are driven out with the Chi-Rho, and the camera surfaces as
    the town's border traces. 13:51 shakes the dust off it.
- **The Jerusalem card** (13:27–37). The rulers, with the Chi-Rho standing
  before them. Pilate on his seat beside an empty balance, and the cross rising
  on the mound outside the gate. The Chi-Rho is taken out to the cross and down
  from it into the tomb, and the stone rolls shut. At 13:30 the stone rolls back
  and the Chi-Rho rises. The witnesses come down the road from Galilee. Then the proof:
  - "Jesus" is named beside the Chi-Rho;
  - the camera sweeps to David's sealed tomb among his fathers';
  - an arc runs from it to the Chi-Rho;
  - David's tomb cracks and grows over ("saw decay");
  - the two tombs stand in one frame ("saw no decay").
- **The Lystra card** (14:8–20), the first of the scene cards: the
  lame man sits, leaps up and walks; the crowd comes in and lifts its arms;
  plinths lift Barnabas and Paul and the names Jupiter and Mercury appear on
  them; the priest leads garlanded oxen down from the temple; the two tear their
  clothes and run in among the people; the names are struck ("We also are men");
  the sun, birds, sheep, paths, rain and harvest come in for the speech of
  14:15–17; Paul is stoned in the gateway, dragged out past the temple, and
  rises with the disciples round him. The camera surfaces at 14:20 for the road
  to Derbe.

## Decisions (the owner, 2026-10-24)

- **Prototype Lystra first**, then build the other two cards: done after the
  owner approved the Lystra prototype.
- **Jesus is shown as the Chi-Rho** (`svg_figures.chi_rho`), the X-and-P
  monogram of Christos, never as a human figure. It comes down the Jordan valley
  (13:25), stands in Jerusalem, and is carried to Paul and Barnabas as "a light
  for the Gentiles" (13:46–47). It is never pulsed. The owner chose it from nine
  sketched options (a light in a ring, which was built first, a plain light, the
  morning star, a gold cross, the cross in a halo, the Chi-Rho, the fish, the
  Lamb, a figure of light). The sketches are in the archive
  (`mockups/acts1314-camera-2026-10-24/emblems`). The manger at 13:23 keeps its
  own light, as in Matthew 1.
- **A figure changes pose only on its verse's narrated event** (Paul stands,
  falls, rises; the lame man leaps; the stone rolls). Nothing re-animates as
  decoration.
- **The map is hand-encoded from named places.** Every anchor is one place's
  coordinate (Wikidata, which is CC0, or Wikipedia); the line between anchors is
  drawn by hand. No line geometry is taken from OpenStreetMap or any other map.

## How the scene card works

A card is a side-view picture hung in the map's world at its town: one card px
is one screen px when the camera's scale is `S_CARD` (200, which is 20,000
px/deg). The camera flies into it with the same van Wijk arcs as any other
flight. Over zooms 0.06–0.16 the card fades in over the town's dot (a
cartouche opening out of the map). Past zoom 0.4 it fills the frame, so the map
is hidden and the compass and scale bar fade out, and the inset keeps a 6 px
you-are-here square.

Its cast are `Actor`s with a start state. Each verse's events (show, move, hop,
pose, ink, rot, grow, grow_x, scale, pulse, label, name_dy, strike, mark)
animate that verse. `Card.state_at` replays the earlier verses so each file
opens where the last one ended. Names hold one screen size and ride their
figure. They come in only from zoom 0.5, so they never cover a card still too
small to read. A name that a verse's rest would leave cut by the frame fades
out early, and comes back in on the next verse. Repeated drawings (a crowd) are
`<use>`s of one drawing per file.

The camera goes from card to card through the map (13:38, Jerusalem to the
synagogue, a 5.4 s arc over Cyprus). A card is drawn only in the verses whose
flight can see it. At 13:47–48 the synagogue's Chi-Rho has a twin on the map,
pinned where it stands, which fades in as the card fades out, so it keeps a
screen size while the town shrinks to a dot.

## Defaults picked without the owner

- The Via Sebaste for Perga to Antioch of Pisidia (round Lake Burdur and the
  north of Lake Eğirdir), and for Antioch of Pisidia to Iconium. The Adada and
  Philomelium alternatives are kept in `acts_geography.ROADS_ALT`.
- Derbe at Kerti Höyük; Lystra at the Zoldera mound.
- The wilderness route by the traditional southern Sinai; the six judges' towns
  are one each for Othniel, Ehud, Deborah, Gideon, Jephthah and Samson.
- Saul's pin stands a little north of Gibeah, so David's figure at Bethlehem
  leaves his dot clear.
- The story's roads are a rose dashed line, never Paul's gold.
- Names may cross rivers (as on a printed map); they keep clear of coasts,
  routes, other names and dots.
- The cast's names leave the Lystra card when the camera pulls back for the
  sky, the earth and the sea at 14:15. They are struck while the camera is
  still close.
- No group is labelled or colour-coded in the synagogue ("Jews", "Gentiles",
  "chief men"): place, posture and the narration tell them apart, and Paul and
  Barnabas are Jews too. The only group names are "rulers" and "witnesses".
- The synagogue's hall stands three steps up on a podium. Its door shuts at
  13:46 as a slab swinging from its hinge.
- David's tomb is in the City of David (Nehemiah 3:16). Golgotha is outside the
  gate and the garden tomb beyond it, and the road north runs to a "Galilee"
  signpost.

## QC

The generator prints any name it dropped or forced, mid-flight overlaps, a
verse that would land after its narration, and a card or world edge in view.
The rest frames were audited in the browser (no overlapping or edge-cut text)
and reviewed as contact sheets, as were the hand-offs between map and cards.
The verse files run 40–140 KB on the map and 70–380 KB in a card. The deploy's
file count is unchanged at 80.
